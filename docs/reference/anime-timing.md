# Timing ideas from Japanese animation (reference, our own words)

Gathered 2026-11-03 for `specs/addon/npc-attack-tempo.md`. Principles only; no copied material.

- **Tame (溜め)**: a held charge right before a big action. The pose sits, then releases. Too much drags the tempo.
- **Tsume (ツメ)**: in-betweens bunched near a key pose, so motion is slow at the extremes and fast in the middle.
  *Saki-zume* bunches at the start (slow leaving), *ato-zume* at the end (slow arriving), *ryō-zume* both. Japanese
  animation pushes this harder than Western easing; even spacing reads flat. In 3D studios the two words are used the
  other way round (tame = lengthen a section, tsume = shorten it).
- **Meri-hari (メリハリ)**: contrast between slow and fast is what reads as power, not overall speed.
- **Yoshida Toru (mecha/effects animator)**: weight is the speed contrast around the impact. A short "don" reads light,
  a long "doooon" reads heavy. Uniform-speed 3D motion feels weightless; a heavy thing should press into a hard
  deceleration before it stops. A throw needs three drawings that each read: wound up, mid, end. Missiles he times on
  fours. He plans timing by onomatopoeia.
- **Koma-uchi (コマ打ち)**: holding each drawing for two or three frames, single frames for key action. Arc System Works
  used this in 3D so the models read as drawn.
- **Kime (決め)**: a few extreme poses that read on their own carry the action.
- **Sakurai Masahiro (Smash Bros.)**: freeze the action for a few frames on impact; longer for stronger hits; also for
  other big moments; brief slow motion then snap back.
- **Obake (オバケ)**: smear frames and speed lines for the fastest part of a move rather than more in-betweens.
